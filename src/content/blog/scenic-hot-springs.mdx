<p>Few soaking spots in Washington have a reputation like Scenic Hot Springs. Tucked into the Cascade Mountains near Stevens Pass, this privately owned spring rewards a steep uphill hike with warm mineral water and views over a forested valley. I consider it one of the best half-day hot spring trips from Seattle.</p><p>What makes Scenic different is how you visit. The springs sit on private property, and the owners protect the place with a reservation system that keeps crowds small. If you plan ahead and come prepared, you get a peaceful soak that feels far wilder than it actually is.</p>

<h2 id="about-scenic-hot-springs">About Scenic Hot Springs</h2><p>Scenic Hot Springs sits on roughly 40 acres of private forest land about 10 miles east of Skykomish, just off US Highway 2 on the way to Stevens Pass. The property borders the Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest, and the springs look down over the Skykomish River valley.</p><p>The hot water here is piped from a natural geothermal source into a small number of soaking pools built into the hillside. The water is unchlorinated and typically hovers around 100–105°F, warm enough for a long soak on a cold day. Because the pools are small and visitor numbers are capped each day, the atmosphere stays quiet.</p><p>The springs have a long history of informal use, and at one point the property was closed entirely because of overuse and trash. The current owners reopened access through a managed reservation system, and that system is the only legal way to visit.</p>

<h2 id="location-and-accessibility">Location and Accessibility</h2><p>Scenic Hot Springs is about 2 hours from Seattle by car, depending on traffic over Stevens Pass. The drive follows US-2 east through Monroe and Skykomish into the mountains.</p><h3 id="getting-there">Getting There</h3><ul><li>Drive US Highway 2 to a point about 10 miles east of the town of Skykomish</li><li>Get the exact trailhead location and parking instructions with your reservation confirmation—the owners do not publish the trailhead publicly</li><li>Book in advance at scenichotsprings.com, since walk-ins are not permitted</li><li>Choose between public access, where you share the springs with other visitors, or private access, where your group reserves the springs exclusively</li><li>Save your confirmation offline, as cell service is unreliable to nonexistent near the trailhead</li></ul><h3 id="what-to-expect-on-the-trail">What to Expect on the Trail</h3><ul><li>Hike roughly 2 miles one way with about 1,100 feet of elevation gain</li><li>Expect a steep, sustained climb on a rugged forest trail rather than a graded path</li><li>Budget 60–90 minutes uphill for most hikers, faster on the way down</li><li>In winter, plan on snowshoes or skis—the route becomes a legitimate backcountry snow climb</li><li>Carry out everything you carry in; there are no trash services at the springs or trailhead</li></ul><p>I always check the weather and recent trip reports before going. Rain makes the trail slick, and winter storms can drop fresh snow on the route with little warning.</p>

<h2 id="facilities-and-amenities">Facilities and Amenities</h2><p>Scenic Hot Springs is a minimally developed facility, and the owners are upfront about that. You come here for the water and the setting, not for resort comforts.</p><h3 id="pools-and-water-temperature">Pools and Water Temperature</h3><p>The springs feed a small number of natural stone pools and tubs arranged on the hillside. Spring water is piped directly into the pools, unchlorinated and untreated, at soaking temperatures around 100–105°F. The upper pools tend to run warmest. Each pool fits only a handful of people, which is exactly why reservations are limited.</p><p>There is a basic changing area and a simple toilet, but no running water, no showers, and no staff on site. Do not leave valuables visible in your car at the trailhead.</p>

<h3 id="rules-and-etiquette">Rules and Etiquette</h3><p>Access to Scenic Hot Springs exists only because visitors follow the rules. I never show up without a reservation—walk-ins are turned away, and the trailhead is on private land. Group sizes are limited, and quiet behavior is expected so everyone gets the peaceful soak they came for.</p><p>Clothing is optional at the pools, so I give others space and keep conversation at a low volume. Glass containers and alcohol stay out of the pool area. No soap, shampoo, or scented products go in the water. I pack out every piece of trash, including food scraps, because past abuse nearly cost everyone access to this place.</p>

<h2 id="tips-for-first-time-visitors">Tips for First-Time Visitors</h2><p>A little preparation makes the difference between a great soak and a miserable hike. Here is what I focus on.</p><h3 id="what-to-bring">What to Bring</h3><ul><li>Sturdy footwear: waterproof hiking boots with real tread for the steep trail; snowshoes or skis in winter</li><li>Weather layers: a rain shell and warm synthetic layers, even in summer—the pass weather changes fast</li><li>Water and snacks: at least 1–2 liters of water, since there is no drinkable water on site</li><li>Towel and swim attire: a compact quick-dry towel; a suit if you prefer one, though clothing is optional</li><li>Reservation confirmation: saved offline or printed, since there is no cell service</li><li>Headlamp: essential if your reservation window runs toward dusk, especially in fall and winter</li><li>Trekking poles: helpful on the steep descent, and on snow</li><li>Trash bag: for packing out everything you bring</li></ul><h3 id="safety-and-considerations">Safety and Considerations</h3><ul><li>Tell someone your plans; the trailhead area has no reliable cell coverage</li><li>Limit soak time and hydrate—hot water plus a steep hike out can leave you lightheaded</li><li>Lock your car and leave nothing valuable visible at the trailhead</li><li>In winter, carry the ten essentials and check avalanche and weather forecasts for the Stevens Pass area</li><li>Respect your reservation window so the next group gets the same experience you did</li></ul>

<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2><p>Scenic Hot Springs earns its popularity. The combination of a steep forest hike, warm unchlorinated pools, and a crowd kept small by reservations makes it one of the most rewarding soaks within day-trip distance of Seattle. Book ahead, pack light but smart, follow the rules, and you will understand why people protect this place so fiercely.</p><p>You can also find Scenic Hot Springs on our <a href="/springs/scenic-hot-springs">hot springs directory page</a> along with other Washington soaks worth planning a trip around.</p>
