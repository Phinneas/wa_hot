Here's the fastest way to choose a Washington hot spring: pick your difficulty first, then pick your spring. Every rating below comes from a real trail stat, not a vibe. Distance and elevation are pulled from the Washington Trails Association, the National Park Service, the Forest Service, or the official site of each spring. Access data is current as of September 2026, and conditions change, so check the live WTA or agency page before every trip.

Three safety notes apply to this whole list. Wild pools are not tested or treated, and the parks warn they can carry harmful bacteria. Backcountry soaks may require permits and bear canisters. And fire, flood, and road closures can shut an approach with no notice.

## Beginner: walk up and soak

Short walks under half a mile or full drive-up access. Family friendly, no special skills needed.

**Sol Duc Hot Springs Resort.** Drive-up, developed, maintained pools inside Olympic National Park. The easiest real hot spring experience in the state. Source: NPS. Who this is for: first-timers and families. [Sol Duc guide](/blog/sol-duc-hot-springs)

**Carson Hot Springs Resort.** Drive-up historic bathhouse in the Columbia River Gorge, soaking by the hour. Source: Washington State Tourism. Who this is for: anyone who wants history with zero hiking. [Carson guide](/blog/carson-hot-springs-complete-visitor-guide)

**Tenzen Springs & Cabins.** Drive-up, no hike at all, but overnight guests only and adults only. Source: tenzensprings.com. Who this is for: couples wanting a private tub and a designed retreat.

**Baker Hot Springs.** A short forest walk of about 0.3 miles, nearly flat. The catch is the drive. Forest Road 1130 is rough, potholed, and snow-closed much of the year, so budget your effort for the car, not the trail. Source: WTA trip reports. Who this is for: beginners with a suitable vehicle and patience for gravel. [Baker guide](/blog/baker-hot-springs-washington-state)

Prep checklist for this tier: swimsuit, towel, water, and a plan to pack out your trash. That's genuinely it.

## Intermediate: earn it a little

Half a mile to 3 miles, moderate elevation, or a seasonal hazard that demands timing and judgment.

**Scenic Hot Springs.** About 2 miles one way with roughly 1,100 feet of elevation gain on a steep forest trail. Private property, reservation required, no walk-ins. In winter the route becomes a snowshoe or ski climb. Source: scenichotsprings.com. Who this is for: reasonably fit hikers who plan ahead. [Scenic guide](/blog/scenic-hot-springs)

**Wind River Hot Springs.** The distance is short, but this one is hazard-gated, not distance-gated. Reaching the pools means crossing the Wind River, which is only safe at low water, roughly July through September. One flag for honesty: no WTA or agency page documents this route, so trail stats here rest on guide reports rather than Tier-1 sources. Difficulty rating is confident on the hazard, less so on the approach. Source: Hot Springs Guides and regional guide reports. Who this is for: confident, conditions-aware visitors in late summer only. [Wind River guide](/blog/wind-river-hot-springs)

Prep checklist for this tier: everything above, plus sturdy footwear, layers, offline directions, and an honest read of conditions before you commit.

## Advanced: a real backcountry day

Three to 6 miles, significant elevation or logistics, self-supported travel.

**Goldmyer Hot Springs.** A 4.5-mile hike in, permit required, entry capped at 20 visitors per day, no cell service, pack in and pack out everything. The trail is moderate but the logistics are real. Source: goldmyer.org. Who this is for: organized hikers ready for a managed wilderness day. [Goldmyer guide](/blog/goldmyer-hot-springs)

Prep checklist for this tier: add the ten essentials, a printed permit, a first-aid kit, and a check-in plan with someone at home.

## Expert: remote, long, or both

Six-plus miles one way or genuine backcountry conditions. Route-finding, fitness, and judgment required.

**Olympic Hot Springs.** About 10.6 miles one way with close to 3,900 feet of gain, rated Hard by WTA. The road is closed to vehicles, so most people bike the 9-mile road section and hike the final 1.5 miles. Untreated pools, wilderness rules, bear canister required for camping. Source: WTA and NPS. Who this is for: strong hikers or bike-packers comfortable with a 20-mile day. [Olympic access guide](/blog/olympic-hot-springs-access)

**Gamma Hot Springs.** Deep in the Glacier Peak Wilderness with no maintained trail to the springs. This is a remote, serious objective. As of September 2026 the Pacific Crest Trail is closed from Suiattle Pass to Mica Lake by the Miners Fire, which affects the usual approach, so check the live USFS and PCTA closure pages before planning anything. Source: USGS and USFS. Who this is for: experienced backcountry travelers only, and not this month. [Gamma guide](/blog/gamma-hot-springs)

**Sulphur Hot Springs.** Also deep in the Glacier Peak Wilderness, and currently harder to reach than usual. The Suiattle River Road washed out in December 2025 and is closed through at least the 2026 season, and the access trail is closed with fire damage. Source: USGS and USFS. Who this is for: nobody right now. Check road status before this one re-enters your plans.

Prep checklist for this tier: ten essentials, bear canister where required, navigation tools with a paper backup, water treatment, and a written trip plan left with a contact. If any of that sounds excessive, pick a lower tier.

## Closed: do not attempt

No difficulty rating, no route, no coordinates. These are listed so you stop planning, not so you start.

**Kennedy Hot Springs.** Buried by the October 2003 flood. The spring is gone. Source: WTA and USFS. [The full story](/blog/kennedy-hot-springs)

**Green River and Lester Hot Springs.** Real springs inside Tacoma's locked municipal watershed. Visiting is illegal and you can be ticketed. Source: WTA and Tacoma Public Utilities. [The full story](/blog/lester-green-river-hot-springs)

**Ohanapecosh Hot Springs.** A protected thermal feature inside Mount Rainier National Park. Look, learn, but no soaking. Source: NPS. [Ohanapecosh guide](/blog/ohanapecosh-hot-springs)
