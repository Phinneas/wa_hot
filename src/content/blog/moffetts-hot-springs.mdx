<p>If you search for Moffett's Hot Springs today, you're looking for a piece of Washington history that is very much alive. This mineral spring on the north bank of the Columbia River Gorge has drawn soakers for well over a century, and after years of closure it recently welcomed visitors again as Bonneville Hot Springs Resort & Spa in North Bonneville, Washington.</p><p>I find Moffett's fascinating because it sits at the crossroads of old and new. Indigenous peoples visited these waters for centuries, a Cascade Hotel once drew crowds here in the 1880s, and today the same geothermal source feeds a modern wellness resort about an hour from Portland. Here's the full story and how to plan a visit.</p>

<h2 id="about-moffetts-hot-springs">About Moffett's Hot Springs</h2><p>Moffett's Hot Springs sits near North Bonneville in Skamania County, on the Washington side of the Columbia River Gorge. The spring is officially recognized by the USGS Geographic Names Information System at roughly 45.656°N, 121.961°W, just off State Route 14 near Bonneville Dam.</p><p>The spring's written history begins around 1880, when a miner named R. J. Snow found hot mineral water emerging along the riverbank—a site local Native American tribes had known and used for centuries. Snow shared the discovery with Thomas Moffett, who recognized its potential and built the Cascade Hotel on the site in 1881. The place became known as Moffett's Hot Springs, and for decades it operated as a destination resort for visitors seeking the supposed healing properties of the mineral-rich water.</p><p>The resort era faded in the twentieth century, but the spring never stopped flowing. In 2003 the site was redeveloped as Bonneville Hot Springs Resort & Spa, blending the historic source with modern amenities. The resort closed in 2016 and was nearly converted into a rehabilitation facility, but new owners brought hospitality back—the <a href="https://www.bonnevilleresorts.com/history-of-bonneville">resort reopened on February 1, 2025</a>, and the springs are once again open to overnight guests.</p>

<h2 id="location-and-accessibility">Location and Accessibility</h2><p>Unlike most springs I cover, Moffett's requires no hike at all. The site is a drive-up resort at 1252 E Cascade Drive in North Bonneville, Washington, roughly an hour east of Portland and about four hours from Seattle via I-5 and SR-14.</p><h3 id="getting-there">Getting There</h3><ul><li>From Portland, take I-84 east and cross to the Washington side near Cascade Locks, or follow SR-14 east through Vancouver and Washougal</li><li>From Seattle, take I-5 south to Longview, then SR-14 east through the Gorge</li><li>The resort sits just west of Bonneville Dam, a convenient landmark</li><li>Book ahead through the resort—the property operates as a lodging and spa destination rather than a drop-in public pool</li><li>Check directly with the resort about day-use soaking availability if you're not staying overnight, as policies have evolved since the 2025 reopening</li></ul>

<h2 id="facilities-and-amenities">Facilities and Amenities</h2><p>The natural geothermal source—around 100°F mineral water—feeds the resort's soaking pools and spa facilities. The setting is forested and quiet, tucked against the hills of the Gorge with Bonneville Dam and Beacon Rock nearby.</p><p>Today's Bonneville Hot Springs Resort & Spa offers mineral soaking, hydrotherapy-focused spa treatments, guest rooms (some with private mineral hot tubs), a restaurant, and a lobby anchored by a 45-foot river stone fireplace. It's a full-service retreat rather than a rustic soak—expect swimsuits, reservations, and resort pricing rather than a free backcountry pool.</p>

<h3 id="rules-and-etiquette">Rules and Etiquette</h3><p>Because Moffett's is now a private resort, the etiquette is simple: book through the resort, follow their pool and spa policies, and wear a swimsuit. There's no public access to the natural spring source itself—the geothermal water reaches visitors through the resort's facilities.</p>

<h2 id="tips-for-first-time-visitors">Tips for First-Time Visitors</h2><h3 id="what-to-bring">What to Bring</h3><ul><li>Swimsuit and sandals for the pools and spa</li><li>A good book—the resort is built for slow, quiet relaxation</li><li>Hiking shoes if you plan to explore; Beacon Rock, Hamilton Mountain, and Dog Mountain are all nearby</li><li>Reservations for both lodging and spa treatments, especially on weekends</li></ul><h3 id="making-the-most-of-the-area">Making the Most of the Area</h3><ul><li>Pair your soak with a Gorge hike—Beacon Rock State Park is minutes away</li><li>Visit Bonneville Dam and its fish ladder, just up the road</li><li>Consider a second soak stop at nearby Carson Hot Springs Resort, the historic Saint Martin's springs, ten minutes east</li><li>Support the reopening: the resort relaunched in 2025 after years of closure, and visits help keep this historic site in hospitality use</li></ul>

<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2><p>Moffett's Hot Springs is proof that a great spring can survive changing times. From Indigenous gathering place to 1880s resort hotel to its current life as Bonneville Hot Springs Resort & Spa, the water has never stopped flowing. If you want a historic Washington soak with resort comfort and zero hiking required, this is one of the easiest hot springs trips in the Columbia River Gorge.</p><p>You can also find Moffett's Hot Springs on our <a href="/springs/moffetts-hot-springs">hot springs directory page</a> along with other Washington soaks worth planning a trip around.</p>
