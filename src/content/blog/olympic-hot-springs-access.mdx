Here's the truth up front. Olympic Hot Springs still exists, still flows, and people still soak there. But you can't drive to it anymore. The road washed out years ago, and reaching the pools now means a 20-mile round trip on foot or bike. And once you get there, the park itself warns the water may not be safe. If that doesn't scare you off, this is one of the wildest soaks in Washington. Here's exactly what you're signing up for.

A quick note before we go further. If you searched for "Enchanted Valley hot springs," you were chasing a ghost. Enchanted Valley is a real and stunning backcountry destination in Olympic National Park, but there's no hot spring there. The soak people actually mean when they talk about wild springs in this corner of the park is [Olympic Hot Springs](/blog/olympic-hot-springs-guide), in the Elwha Valley. That's what this guide covers.

## What happened to the road

Olympic Hot Springs used to be an easy visit. You'd drive most of the way in. Not anymore.

The Olympic Hot Springs Road in the Elwha Valley is closed to vehicles at the Madison Falls parking area because of a washout. That closure has been in place for years, and the National Park Service still lists it as active. The road is open to people on foot, on bikes, and to dogs on leash for the road portion, but the gate is where your car stops.

So the springs didn't go anywhere. The easy way in did.

## The trip, step by step

From Madison Falls, here's the actual route.

You travel about 9 miles along the closed road to reach the Boulder Creek Trailhead. A bypass trail routes you around the washout itself, which sits less than a mile in. You can hike this stretch or bike it, and a lot of people bike it to save time.

From the Boulder Creek Trailhead, you hike a final 1.5 miles on a footpath to reach the springs. No bikes are allowed on that last trail section, so if you rode in, you lock up and walk the rest.

All told, Washington Trails Association clocks it at about 10.6 miles one way, close to 3,900 feet of elevation gain, and rates it Hard. A hiker who did it in April 2026 logged nearly 20 miles round trip and found the trail in good shape, with a single creek crossing.

If you have a bike, use it. The same hiker noted the ride made the trip out dramatically faster.

## The pools themselves

Olympic Hot Springs is not a developed site. There's no resort here anymore, no concrete, no attendant. What remains are natural, unmaintained hot water seeps scattered through the forest of the Elwha Valley wilderness.

You'll find several pools. Some sit right along the trail. Others are tucked away and take a little looking. Temperatures vary a lot from pool to pool, and some run as hot as 118 degrees, so test the water with a hand before you climb in.

They still flow. As recently as late April 2026, a visitor soaked and filed a report, though they noted a couple of the pools looked a little green. Which brings us to the part you cannot skip.

## The warning you have to take seriously

The National Park Service is blunt about this, and so am I.

These pools are in a wilderness area. The park does not test them and does not treat them. They may contain harmful bacteria, and using them is at your own risk. WTA passes along the park's specific caution that the water may hold high levels of fecal coliform bacteria.

That's not a formality. This is untreated surface water sitting in warm rock. Plenty of people soak anyway, but you should go in knowing exactly what the park is telling you. Keep your head above water, avoid going in with open cuts, and make your own informed call. I'm not going to pretend the risk isn't there.

## If you want to camp or stay out longer

Camping is permitted near the springs, but there are rules.

You need a wilderness use permit before your trip. You also need a hard-sided bear canister. The park no longer allows the old bear-wire hanging system, so a canister is now required. The campground near the springs is the one the park now calls Boulder Creek, though older signs still say Hot Springs Campground. Altair and other campgrounds along the road are closed to overnight stays.

There are no services past the Madison Falls gate. Pack out everything.

## The easy alternative, if this is too much

Not everyone wants a 20-mile day for a soak in questionable water. Fair.

If you want the Olympic Peninsula hot springs experience without the effort or the risk, go to [Sol Duc Hot Springs Resort](/blog/sol-duc-hot-springs) instead. The pools there are developed, maintained, and easy to reach by car when the resort is open for the season. It's a completely different day, warm, tested water and no bacteria gamble, and it's the honest recommendation for most visitors.

## One thing that might change

The Park Service has an active environmental assessment to rehabilitate the washed-out road. If that work moves forward, access to Olympic Hot Springs could get easier down the line. So before you plan a trip, check the current NPS road and conditions pages. The situation I've described is accurate now, but this is a place where "now" has an expiration date.
